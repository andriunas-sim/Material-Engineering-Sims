Precipitation Hardening — Lattice Model

A substitutional alloy (think Al–Cu) on a fixed crystal lattice. Every site holds a matrix atom, a solute atom, or a vacancy. Atoms only move by swapping with a neighbouring vacancy. Solute–solute bonds lower the energy, so after quenching the solute clusters, the clusters grow, and the large ones eat the small ones. Hardness is calculated from the resulting microstructure.

How the model works

Lattice. 120 × 80 square lattice with periodic boundaries. Site occupancy is matrix, solute or vacancy. Lattice sites never move, only their occupants change. This is the substitutional picture: Cu replaces Al on the FCC lattice.

Energetics. Each solute–solute nearest-neighbour bond lowers the energy by ε. All other pairs contribute nothing. This is the lattice-gas (Ising) model of a system with a miscibility gap.

Kinetics (vacancy mechanism). A random vacancy tries to swap with a random neighbour. Swapping with a matrix atom is always accepted. Swapping with a solute atom is accepted with the Metropolis probability:

P = min[1, exp(−ΔE / kT)],   ΔE = ε (bonds broken − bonds made)

So a solute atom readily joins a cluster but rarely leaves one at low temperature. Real time accumulates as Δt ∝ 1 / (Nv Γ), with jump rate Γ ∝ exp(−Em/kT) and Em = 2ε. Lower temperature therefore means a slower clock, and more vacancies mean a faster one.

Solvus. The equilibrium solubility comes from Onsager's exact 2D Ising result:

ceq = ½ [1 − (1 − sinh−4(ε / 2kT))1/8]

Above the solvus for the chosen composition, clusters dissolve. That is solution treatment.

Hardness. A precipitate is any connected solute cluster of 4 or more atoms. Let f be the fraction of sites in precipitates, r̄ the atom-weighted mean radius (in lattice spacings), and css the solute still in solution. Three contributions are combined:

Δτshear ∝ √(f r̄),   ΔτOrowan ∝ √f / r̄,   Δτss ∝ css2/3

Δτppt = (Δτshear−4 + ΔτOrowan−4)−1/4

Whichever mechanism is weaker (easier for the dislocation) controls. The crossover radius rc = 2.8 lattice spacings (about 25 atoms) sets the peak. Clusters below rc are drawn yellow (sheared) and those above are drawn red (looped).

Limitations. The model is 2D and isotropic, so GP zones appear as compact blobs rather than {100} discs. Every cluster sits on matrix sites, so it is always coherent. The GP → θ″ → θ′ → θ sequence and loss of coherency are not modelled, and the shear-to-Orowan transition is set by size alone. Vacancy numbers stay constant: there is no annihilation at sinks and no solute–vacancy binding.
