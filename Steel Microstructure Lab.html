Model notes and assumptions
  • Phase boundaries (A3, Acm, A1 = 727 °C, eutectoid 0.76 %C, α solubility 0.022 %C, Fe₃C 6.67 %C) are piecewise fits to the Fe–Fe₃C diagram used in the lecture. Mode 1 assumes equilibrium: fractions come straight from the lever rule, and the eutectoid reaction is shown as an isothermal hold at 727 °C.
  • Mode 2 uses JMAK kinetics, X = 1 − exp(−b·tⁿ), with n and b fitted at each temperature to approximate 1 % and 99 % curves for plain 0.76 %C steel (nose ≈ 540 °C, under 1 s). Continuous cooling uses Scheil additivity on the isothermal curves. Diffusional transformation is stopped below 450 °C, where the remaining austenite waits for Ms.
  • Martensite uses Koistinen–Marburger, f = 1 − exp(−0.011(Ms − T)) with Ms = 220 °C. This leaves about 11 % retained austenite at 20 °C, because Mf for 0.76 %C lies below room temperature.
  • Hardness is a rule-of-mixtures estimate (martensite HB 700, as on the slide), ±15 %. Use it for comparison, not for design.
